**Bounded recommendation**
Use a qualification gate, not a migration choice, as the next step:
1. Require each vendor to confirm both capabilities for your exact plan/tier in one place: regional failover behavior, failover target region rules, restore granularity, restore RTO/RPO, and whether tenant-only restore works with your tenancy model.
2. Reject any vendor that cannot show both capabilities in current docs or written support confirmation inside your six-week window.
3. If both remain ambiguous, defer migration rather than building around undocumented behavior. For a solo maintainer on this timeline, that ambiguity is the real risk.

**Alternative and tradeoff**
A stricter alternative is to self-manage Postgres DR and per-tenant restore mechanics. You gain control and clearer semantics; you give up schedule safety and likely the `$100/month` target once backups, standby, monitoring, and restore automation are counted. For this project, that is probably the wrong first move unless both vendors fail qualification.

**Failure conditions**
This recommendation becomes wrong if either vendor produces clear, current, plan-specific documentation or written confirmation proving both mandatory features, including the restore scope you need for your tenancy model. It also changes if your actual tenancy model only supports full-cluster restore, because then “tenant-isolated restore” may require application-level tooling regardless of vendor.

**Next action and proof**
First action: send a short capability checklist to both vendors and ask for plan-specific confirmation.
Acceptance behavior: you can mark one vendor “eligible” only if both mandatory boxes are explicitly satisfied.
Focused check: ask for the exact restore workflow for one tenant and the exact failover workflow for a regional outage, including what is manual vs automatic.
Escalation signal: if either answer is vague, contradictory, or depends on higher-tier pricing that breaks the `$100/month` cap, stop the migration evaluation for that vendor.
