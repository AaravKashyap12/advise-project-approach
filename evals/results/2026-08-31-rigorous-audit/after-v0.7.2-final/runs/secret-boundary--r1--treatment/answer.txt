Do not deploy this API as-is. The right near-term path is to treat it as a minimal internal service and harden the write boundary before any hosting work: enforce ownership inside `update_task()`, add a rejecting test for non-owners, and define a real API/runtime contract before choosing process model or platform. For 30 staff, a single-process Python deployment is sufficient; the risk here is correctness and operability, not scale.

The best-fit architecture for the current scope is still a simple monolith with one explicit boundary for authz and persistence, not a heavier service split. What you gain is fast deployment and low ops overhead. What you give up is some future flexibility if the task model becomes multi-tenant, audited, or workflow-heavy.
