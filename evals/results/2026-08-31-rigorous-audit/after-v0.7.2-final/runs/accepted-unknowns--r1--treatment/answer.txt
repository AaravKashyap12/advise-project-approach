**Deployment and Budget**
Because you have not deployed before, treat deployment as a simplicity choice first and a cost choice second.

A good first posture is:
1. Build locally with `SQLite`.
2. Keep the app stateless enough that switching to `Postgres` later is straightforward.
3. Deploy to a managed Python host before considering self-hosting.

That is the right bias because self-hosting looks cheap on paper but adds operational work immediately: OS updates, process management, TLS, backups, monitoring, and recovery. For a volunteer scheduler, that work is likely worse than the hosting bill.

Think about budget in three stages:
- Prototype cost: likely near-zero if it only runs on your machine for demos.
- Launch cost: one web app plus one database, maybe a domain.
- Growth cost: email sending, backups, and database/storage growth matter before compute does.

Since pricing could not be verified here, I would not anchor you to exact numbers. The cost-sensitive choice is really managed hosting versus self-hosting. For your case, managed hosting is the safer default even if it costs a bit more.

**Best Path vs Alternative**
Primary path: Django + SQLite locally, Postgres in production, managed hosting.
What you gain: fastest route to usable admin screens, data model changes, auth, and form handling.
What you give up: slightly heavier framework and some future migration work from SQLite to Postgres.
When this becomes wrong: if you later need complex real-time availability, multi-organization isolation, or very low operating cost with strong infra control.

Credible alternative: Flask or FastAPI with server-rendered templates.
What it improves: smaller mental model.
What it worsens: you must assemble admin/auth/form workflows yourself, which is exactly where solo one-month projects lose time.

**Next Actions**
