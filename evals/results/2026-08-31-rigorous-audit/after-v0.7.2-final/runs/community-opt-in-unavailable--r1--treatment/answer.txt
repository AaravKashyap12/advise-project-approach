Constraint fit: the main drivers are speed, simplicity, and budget discipline. `FastAPI + SQLite FTS5` is strong here because you can ship search, auth-lite, admin upload/edit flows, and basic relevance quickly in plain Python. For 50 staff and handbook-style text, SQLite is usually enough for MVP if the corpus is modest and mostly read-heavy. Store source content as Markdown files or a simple table with rendered HTML cached on write; avoid a CMS, vector DB, Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, or microservices in v1 unless a real requirement appears.

The best credible alternative is Django with PostgreSQL full-text search. It improves built-in admin, auth, and long-term multi-user content management, but it worsens setup complexity and hosting overhead for a four-week MVP. Another alternative is a static-site generator plus client-side search index; that is cheaper and simpler to host, but it becomes worse if you need permissions, editorial workflow, usage tracking, or frequent updates without rebuild friction. I would not start with embeddings or semantic search here: you gain fuzzy retrieval, but you give up cost predictability, simplicity, and debuggability, and the content type does not justify it yet.

What I would build first:
1. Ingestion pipeline: load handbook pages from Markdown or uploaded docs into normalized records with title, section, tags, body, and updated timestamp.
Acceptance: a changed source document appears in the app with updated metadata and body.
Focused check: edit one document, re-run ingest, and confirm only that record changes and old search results disappear.

2. Search slice: index the content with SQLite FTS5 and expose keyword search with snippet highlights, section filters, and sort by relevance.
Acceptance: staff can search a term and reach the right page within one click.
Focused check: prepare 10 known queries against seeded handbook content and verify the expected page appears in the top results for most of them.

3. Lightweight internal delivery: simple login or network-only access, page view, search analytics, and an admin refresh endpoint or scheduled ingest.
Acceptance: a staff user can sign in or access internally, search, open pages, and admins can publish updates without touching code.
Focused check: test from two browsers and confirm search, page rendering, and content refresh work end to end.
